Let $G$ be a group and let $g \in G$. The **cyclic subgroup** generated by $g$ is 
$\langle g \rangle = \{ g^m : m \in \mathbb{Z} \}$ 

**Lemma:** Suppose $g$ has order $n$. Then $\langle g \rangle = \{1, g, g^2,...,g^{n-1}\}$ 
Moreover, $1, g, ..., g^{n-1}$ are pairwise distinct, hence $\# \langle g \rangle = n$  ^b21157

We say that $G$ is **cyclic** if $G = \langle g \rangle$ for some $g \in G$, which is called the *generator* of $G$

**Lemma:** Cyclic groups are Abelian
*Proof:* Suppose $G = \langle g \rangle$. Then 
- $g^r \cdot g^s = g^{r+s} = g^{s+r} = g^s \cdot g^r$
	- As integer addition is commutative
