# Sequences and Series of Functions

>[!note] Example
>Let $(f_n)$ given by $f_n : [0, 1] \to \mathbb{R}, f_n(x) = x^\frac{1}{n}$. 
>Note that $f_n(0) = 0$ for all $n$. For every $x \in (0, 1]$, we have $lim_{n \to \infty} x^\frac{1}{n} = 1$. 
>As a result, the limit of the sequence $(f_n)$ is $$f(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & x = 0 \\ 1 & x \in (0, 1] \end{cases}$$

Notice that this shows the pointwise limit of a sequence of continuous function does not have to be continuous.
